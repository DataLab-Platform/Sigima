# Version 1.4 #

## Sigima Version 1.4.0 ##

### Requirements ###

* Python 3.10 or later is now required. Python 3.9 users must keep an earlier compatible release.

### ✨ New features since version 1.3.0 ###

* **Origin of experimental data**: Objects imported from experimental files or captured from instruments can record where their data came from in the new `experimental_source` metadata entry: source kind, file path or instrument address, scan or dataset, selected part, timestamps, applied conversions and other details. The entry is a versioned dictionary made of JSON types only, so it remains readable without the reader or connector that created it, and it is restored with the same Python types by Sigima's native signal and image files (and by DataLab workspaces): a restored entry can be serialized to JSON or stored again. Values are limited to what HDF5 files restore exactly: `None` and keys that are not plain names are rejected, and NumPy scalars are converted to Python values. It is created and accessed with `make_experimental_source`, `set_experimental_source` and `get_experimental_source` (`sigima.objects`). The existing `source` metadata entry is unchanged.
* **Group UUID from the remote client**: `add_group` of the remote client (`sigima.client`) now returns the UUID of the created group, so that objects can be added to it with `group_id=...` (DataLab ≥ 1.4.0; older versions return `None`).

* **Pixel grid conversions**: New `ImageObj` methods convert between physical coordinates and pixel indices consistently with ROIs: `physical_to_pixel_range` and `pixel_range_to_physical` (rectangle and range of pixels), `axis_to_float_indices` and `axis_to_physical` (fractional indices, for uniform and non-uniform coordinates) and `get_roi_crop_indices` (part of the image holding a ROI). `transformer.transform_like_image` (`sigima.proc.image`) moves a geometry result as a flip, a transposition or a 90° or 270° rotation moves the pixels of the image.
* **Removal of file formats**: The I/O registries (`SignalIORegistry`, `ImageIORegistry`) have new `unregister_format` and `register_format` methods, so that a plugin defining file formats can remove them from Sigima when it is unloaded, and register them again, without accessing the registry internals.
* **NPZ object archives**: New functions `serialize_object_to_npz` and `deserialize_object_from_npz` (`sigima.io`) convert a signal or an image to and from an in-memory archive of NumPy arrays and JSON metadata, keeping labels, units, uncertainties, uniform or non-uniform image coordinates and object metadata, without ever pickling arrays. This is the format used by the Web API of DataLab to transfer objects, so that DataLab and its clients (such as DataLab-Kernel) share one implementation.

The [user guide](../user_guide/overview.rst) shows how readers and connectors attach experimental-source metadata, save and restore it, and send an acquisition to a DataLab group. Source recording is opt-in and does not make the experiment reproducible.

### 💥 Breaking changes since version 1.3.0 ###

* **Pixel selection of rectangular ROIs**: A rectangular ROI now selects the pixels whose centers lie inside the rectangle, bounds included, like circular and polygonal ROIs and like the display (pixels are drawn centered on their coordinates). The bounds were rounded to the nearest pixel index, which could shift the selection by up to half a pixel. A rectangle whose bounds lie exactly on pixel centers now includes the pixels on both bounds, i.e. one more row and one more column than before: on an image with `x0 = y0 = 0` and `dx = dy = 1`, the rectangle `[0, 0, 10, 10]` (in physical coordinates) now selects 11 × 11 pixels. The same rule gives the part of the image holding a ROI of any shape, hence the size of the `extract_roi` and `extract_rois` results. A rectangle with no width or no height selects no pixel.
* **Pixel selection of circular and polygonal ROIs**: Circular and polygonal ROIs now also select the pixels whose centers lie inside their shape, boundary included. The vertices of polygons were rounded to the nearest pixel indices, so that the selected pixels could differ from the polygon drawn on screen. Circles excluded the pixels whose centers lie on the circle, and were drawn in index space for non-uniform coordinates, which does not give a circle in physical coordinates.
* **Grids of ROIs**: The cells of ROI grids are now made of whole pixels: each cell holds the pixels whose centers lie in it, from its start included to its end excluded, and its ROI goes from the outer edges of these pixels. Adjacent cells thus never share a pixel, and the cells of a grid with 100 % sizes and steps tile the image exactly; cell positions are rounded to whole pixels. The grid of an image with non-uniform coordinates now spans the actual extent of its axes: its last rows and columns were left out.
* **Rectangular ROIs in pixel indices**: The physical rectangle of a ROI defined in pixel indices now goes from the outer edges of its pixels, half a pixel before the center of the first pixel and half a pixel after the center of the last one. Converting a ROI between pixel indices and physical coordinates keeps the same pixels.
* **ROIs saved with earlier versions**: The ROIs of objects saved with earlier versions are not converted: they select pixels according to the rules above, so that processing and analysis in these ROIs may give different results when they are recomputed.
* **Replay of processing in image ROIs**: The selection of image pixels by regions of interest is now recorded with each call on images carrying regions of interest, and with each call of the operations taking image regions of interest as parameters (`extract_roi`, `extract_rois`, `erase` and `offset_correction`), whatever their input (rule `sigima.image.roi.pixel_centers`, `sigima.proc.contracts.IMAGE_ROI_CONVENTION`, in the execution context described by `describe_call` and `prepare_execution`). Applications that replay recorded processing (e.g. DataLab capsules) refuse to replay such calls recorded with earlier versions, which selected other pixels, instead of reporting a different result. Other calls on images without regions of interest are not concerned.
* **Image center**: `ImageObj.xc` and `ImageObj.yc` are now the center of the pixel grid (halfway between the centers of the first and last pixels), for uniform and non-uniform coordinates: they were half a pixel off for uniform coordinates. `width` and `height` are unchanged.
* **Ellipse coordinates**: `EllipseCoordinates` (`sigima.objects.shape`) now holds the 5 values `(x, y, a, b, theta)` of ellipse geometry results, which can thus be transformed.
* **Operation contract versions**: The operation contracts keep version 1 in this release, although the result of some operations changed since 1.3.0 (for example `resampling`, `extract_roi` and the product of signals): the contracts had just been published with Sigima 1.3.0 and were not used yet. This one-off exception does not apply to later changes: from now on, the version of a contract is increased whenever a change of the computation changes its result, whatever the releases of the applications using Sigima, and calls recorded with an earlier version are refused instead of being replayed (see [Operation contracts and versions](../user_guide/contracts.rst)). The image profiles and the product of signals (`product`), changed afterwards (see below), are thus at version 2.
* **Image profiles in physical coordinates**: The line, segment, average and radial profiles of images are now defined and returned in the physical coordinates of the image, like regions of interest and analysis results, instead of pixel indices. Their parameters were renamed, so that scripts using the former names fail explicitly instead of silently using another meaning: `LineProfileParam.row`/`col` became `y`/`x` (the row or column whose pixel centers are the nearest), `SegmentProfileParam.row1`/`col1`/`row2`/`col2` and `AverageProfileParam.row1`/`col1`/`row2`/`col2` became `x1`/`y1`/`x2`/`y2` (for the average profile, `None` stands for the edge of the image instead of `-1`, and the pixels are those whose centers lie in the rectangle, like rectangular ROIs), and `RadialProfileParam.x0`/`y0` became `xc`/`yc`. The X axis of the profiles holds physical coordinates or distances, with the unit and label of the corresponding axis of the image. Segment profiles are sampled at regular steps from their start point to their end point included, with one sample per pixel along the axis where the segment spans the most pixels (as in Sigima 1.3), each sample taking the value of the nearest pixel. Radial profiles are binned in steps of the smallest distance between pixel centers, so that they are circular for anisotropic pixels and non-uniform coordinates. Segment and radial profiles are refused when the X and Y units of the image differ (and radial profiles when the centers of neighboring pixels coincide). Coordinates outside the image are refused instead of being clamped. On images without calibration (origin 0, pixel size 1), line, average and radial profiles are unchanged, and segment profiles take the same pixels for horizontal, vertical and diagonal segments: other oblique segments now take the nearest pixel of each sample instead of truncating its indices. The contracts of `line_profile`, `segment_profile`, `average_profile` and `radial_profile` are at version 2.

### 🛠️ Bug Fixes since version 1.3.0 ###

* **Image profiles**: The radial profile centered on the image center was computed around a wrong point on calibrated images (the physical center was used as pixel indices), and could even contain undefined values. Vertical profiles were labeled with the unit of the X axis instead of the Y axis, and all profiles with the unit of the image although their X axis was in pixels. Anti-diagonal segment profiles (e.g. from the top-right to the bottom-left corner) were extracted along the other diagonal.
* **Exact integer import**: Integer data that does not fit any integer type supported by the destination object (for example `uint32` counts above 2 147 483 647 in an image, or large integers in a signal) is now converted to a floating-point type that represents every value exactly. Such data was previously converted to `float32` when the source used 4-byte integers, which rounded counts above 16 777 216. A warning is now emitted when no supported floating-point type is exact (integers beyond 2⁵³).
* **Image resampling**: Resampling an image onto its own grid now gives back the same image. The output pixels were sampled half a pixel away from the requested positions, so that every value was shifted and the last row and column were filled with NaN, while the output origin claimed the requested position. The `xmin`/`xmax` and `ymin`/`ymax` parameters of `resampling` are now the coordinates of the centers of the first and last output pixels, like the `xmin`/`xmax` of an image, and default to the source grid. In pixel size mode, the requested pixel size is now kept exactly (the last pixel is the last one not beyond `xmax`/`ymax`).
* **Real and complex signals**: The sum, average, product and standard deviation of several signals, and their conversion into an image, no longer depend on the order of the signals: when a real signal came first, the imaginary parts of the complex signals were discarded.
* **Complex images**: The sum, average and product of several images now accept complex images, which raised an error.
* **Complex image resampling and calibration**: Resampling a complex image with the default fill value (NaN), and the polynomial calibration of the Z axis of a complex image, now keep the imaginary parts: the data was converted to real numbers, even when resampling an image onto its own grid.
* **Independent results**: The results of the transpose, 90° and 270° rotations and horizontal and vertical flips of an image no longer share their data (and their coordinates, for non-uniform images) with the source image: modifying a result in place modified its source too.
* **ROI extraction of non-uniform images**: Extracting several regions of interest of an image with non-uniform coordinates into a single image now gives it the coordinates of the extracted pixels: the X coordinates were taken from the extracted rows and the Y coordinates from the extracted columns.
* **Arbitrary rotation of non-uniform images**: The arbitrary rotation (`rotate`) now rejects images with non-uniform coordinates, like resizing and resampling: the result kept coordinates that did not match its pixels (nor its shape, when the rotation enlarged the image).
* **Uncertainty of a product of signals**: The uncertainty of the product of signals is now propagated where a factor is zero: the result had no uncertainty at all there. Complex factors now give a real uncertainty, computed from their moduli. The uncertainty no longer becomes undefined (NaN) when the factors span a very wide range of magnitudes (e.g. 10³⁰⁸ × 1 × 10⁻³⁰⁸) or when the product itself is too small to be represented, and no longer vanishes when many signals are multiplied (it was wrong, then zero, from about 150 `float32` or 1 000 `float64` signals): it is defined wherever its value can be represented, whatever the number and the order of the signals. The contract of `product` is at version 2.
* **ROIs and annotations after flips and rotations**: Horizontal and vertical flips and 90° and 270° rotations now move the ROIs and annotations of an image with its pixels: they were mirrored about a point half a pixel away from the center of the pixel grid, hence shifted by one pixel, and non-uniform coordinates were ignored. The move is exact for uniform coordinates. For non-uniform coordinates, reversing the pixels along a non-uniform axis is not an affine mapping: points, bounds and unrotated rectangles are moved exactly, while circles, ellipses and polygons are moved approximately (a circle does not stay a circle). ROIs defined in pixel indices were moved as if they were physical coordinates, and inverse ROIs (which mask the inside of their shape) became normal ROIs after these operations, the transposition and the translation.
* **Coordinates of an extracted ROI**: `extract_roi` now gives the extracted image the coordinates of the extracted pixels. The origin of the source image was counted twice, and the coordinates of non-uniform images were shifted instead of being cut to the extracted pixels.
* **Analysis results in ROIs**: The coordinates of the analysis results computed in a ROI (centroid, peaks, contours, etc.) are now offset by the position of the first extracted pixel: they could be off by up to half a pixel when the ROI bounds did not lie on pixel centers.
